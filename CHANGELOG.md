# Changelog

Every change you'd notice as someone making a game with IFLoom is listed here, newest first. The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/), and versions will follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html) once there is a first release.

IFLoom is pre-release, so nothing is a stable API yet and anything below can still change.

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- An engine for any shape of game: a linear novel, a management game played a week at a time, or a life sim with a calendar, places and characters.
- A small core that keeps the turn, scenes and choices, stats, entities, a kv store (keys with a true/false, number or text value), collections, storylines, triggers, notices, inputs and saves.
- Plugins for the rest, each a package of its own: a calendar, places, a cast of characters, quests, a wardrobe, messages, feeds, devices, a ready-made phone, and debug commands that are left out of release builds.
- Content as data. Conditions and effects are plain objects, and your game's own logic plugs in by name with `{ custom: name }`.
- `ifloom check`, which checks a game's content and lists any missing pictures, videos and audio files before anyone plays.
- Every choice is remembered, so a later scene can ask what the player chose.
- Stats you declare yourself, with optional levels, derived stats worked out from a formula, and stats that belong to kinds of entity.
- Seeded randomness. The same save on the same turn gives the same result, so undo can't re-roll and playtests repeat exactly.
- Weighted variants and storylets: scenes drawn at random from a set, each with a weight and its own conditions.
- Saves with undo and redo, a backlog, autosave, export and import, and storage in memory, localStorage or IndexedDB.
- Saves that survive game updates. Give your game a `version` and `migrations`, and older saves load into the new version.
- A profile that outlives saves, settings your game declares, and New Game+.
- Every line the player reads comes from a strings table, so a game or a translation can reword it.
- Mods: data-only files that players add at runtime, checked against the game and recorded in saves.
- The React UI, `@ifloom/react`, with `mountGame` to start a game in one line, a title screen, settings, load and credits pages, and four theme presets: paper, terminal, studio and storybook.
- Ways to make the React UI your own: replace any component, change the classes on any part, drop the styles entirely, or draw your own root.
- Audio: music, ambience, sound effects and voice lines on channels, with volume and mute as settings. `@ifloom/audio` plays it with any framework.
- Media: inline pictures and video, tagged picture pools, layered stages with hotspots, and a lightbox.
- Transitions: a scene, a place or the whole game can fade, slide or zoom in and out, and a choice can say how the next screen arrives with a `transition` effect. A block's `reveal`, or `[[reveal]]` in prose, holds a line back so a scene's text appears one part after another. The engine only names them in `view.transition` and the scene's beats; the React UI draws them with CSS, shows everything at once on a click or a key, and plays nothing when the player asks for reduced motion or the game passes `motion: false`.
- Actions and tooltips in the text. `[[act:id|words]]` draws one of the scene's choices inside a line of prose as a link, and leaves it out of the list; in a room it draws one of the room's buttons. `[[tip:key|words]]` gives a word a note from the prose line `key`. A choice can now send a `command` as well as its effects, such as opening the phone. The view gives each such line `spans`, the React UI draws them as `InlineAction` and `Tip` (focusable, with the reason or note on hover, focus or a tap), the templates and the editor draw them too, and `ifloom check` reports unknown ids, duplicates and malformed markup.
- The CLI: `ifloom new` to start a game from a template, plus `dev`, `build`, `check`, `playtest` and `snapshot`.
- `ifloom build` makes one HTML file that opens from disk, with its pictures beside it, or inside it if you add `--inline-assets`.
- `ifloom playtest`, which plays your game with no one at the keyboard and writes every screen to a transcript.
- A `vanilla` template that draws a game with plain DOM, for Vue, Svelte or no framework at all.
- `vue` and `svelte` templates for `ifloom new`, which draw the blank game with Vue 3 or Svelte 5 and no React: the scene, its choices with the reason a greyed-out one can't be pressed, inputs, stats and a running minigame, with sound from `@ifloom/audio`. The `vanilla` template now draws the same pieces and plays sound too. Using another framework walks through all three side by side, and the HUD, plugin UI, minigame, title screen and audio player pages show Vue and Svelte versions of what they build.
- Three example games: The Letter (a linear novel), The Corner Café (a management game) and Small Town (a life sim that uses every feature).
- Minigames: small games inside the story, such as a quick-time event, declared as states with timers, inputs, their own variables and seeded random picks. Start one with the `minigame` effect or a scene's `minigame` field, and drive it with the `tick` and `minigame` commands. The view shows it as `view.minigame`. Undo, the backlog and autosave step over ticks, and a command log with ticks plays back exactly. The React UI draws it with `MinigamePanel`, which runs the clock and takes the arrow keys and space, and a game can swap in its own panel per minigame through `panels`. `useMinigame()` gives a panel of your own the minigame and its clock.
- `round: 'floor' | 'round' | 'ceil'` on a stat, so it reads as a whole number after plugins' modifiers and its formula, wherever it's read. The kept value isn't rounded. Small Town's energy rounds.
- `display: 'number' | 'bar' | 'pips'` on a stat, passed through the view with the stat's `min` and `max` so a UI can draw a bar or pips. The React UI's stat list draws a basic bar or a row of pips for it.
- Keyboard shortcuts in the React UI, off unless you pass `keys`: the number keys pick the choices on screen, each showing its number, Escape opens the menu, and `keys: { map }` adds keys of your own that send a command.
- `NoticeStrip` in the React UI: a row of unread counts (unseen notices, and apps with a badge) that open where they are. Add it to a layout as `'notice-strip'`.
- `@ifloom/plugin-effects`: timed effects such as "Caffeinated" that last some turns, change what the player's stats read while they last, and run effects as they start, each turn and as they end. Applying one again refreshes it or adds a stack, as you choose per effect, and `effects/remove` lets you say whether its `onEnd` runs.
- `statModifiers` on a plugin: changes to what the player's stats read right now, such as a timed boost, without writing to the stats. Conditions, derived stats, costs and the view all see the changed value.
- `@ifloom/plugin-achievements`: achievements earned the first time a condition holds or by an effect, secret ones shown as `???` until earned, and progress counts such as 4 of 10 read from a stat, a formula, a kv key or a custom value. Each one earned raises a notice, and everything earned in any save is kept in the player's profile, so the view shows it as earned in every new game too.
- `@ifloom/plugin-shop`: vendors that sell items for prices in any spendable stat, with limited stock that restocks each turn or day, selling back at a fraction of the price, and a few goods marked down each day, drawn from the save's seed. A vendor keeps the calendar's hours and stands at a place on the map when the game has those plugins. Its `buy` command works on any button, with the price shown. Small Town's café sells sandwiches both ways: from the counter, and with its plain "Buy a sandwich" activity.
- A plugin's view can read its own namespace of the player's profile as `context.profile`, when a runner builds it. The runner follows the profile, so the view updates when the namespace changes. Conditions and effects still never see the profile, so replays stay exact.
- `spends` on a plugin's command: the stats it spends, so a button that sends it shows its price as an activity's does.
- `@ifloom/plugin-jobs`: jobs with hiring requirements, shifts that cost stats and time and pay a wage in a spendable stat, promotion ladders with better pay, and getting fired for missed shifts or a condition such as a low performance stat. Shifts fall on the calendar's weekdays and periods and are worked at a place on the map when the game has those plugins.
- `@ifloom/plugin-generators`: make new entities from templates, with each field picked from a named pool of values (with optional weights), drawn from a number range, or fixed. Draws are seeded by the save, so a replay makes the same ones.
- A guide, "Coming from SugarCube or Harlowe", that maps Twine's passages, links, macros, special passages, saves, audio and randomness to IFLoom, with side-by-side snippets.
- A guide, "Building UI for plugins", that builds an achievements page, a shop counter and a job board in the React UI from the plugins' views.
- These docs, with search, a version picker, `llms.txt` and `llms-full.txt` for AI agents, every page as Markdown, and an `AGENTS.md` in every new game.
- The editor at `/editor` on the website: write scenes, choices, stats, kv keys, items and storylets from forms in your browser, play them in the React UI as you go with any theme preset, see what `checkGame` finds, and export the pack as TypeScript or JSON to drop into a game. `/editor/snippets` builds a condition, effects, a stat, an item, a setting or prose markup for you to copy.
- Every reference and plugin page in the docs ends with "See it in an example": the file and the part of Small Town, The Letter or The Corner Café that uses it, with a link to the source.
- The Corner Café example's reputation has levels, with a notice when word gets round.
- Diagrams in the docs, drawn with Mermaid: how the engine, your game and your UI fit together, what happens in a command, how a save loads, how plugins join the core, the runner's loop and the quick feet minigame. Each keeps its Mermaid source in the page's Markdown and in `llms-full.txt`.
- The docs are at https://ifloom.dev, with an MCP server at `https://ifloom.dev/api/mcp` that an agent can use to search them and read single pages, and an Open menu on each page that opens it in ChatGPT, Claude or Cursor, or as Markdown.
- Old packs and mods keep loading. A pack written for an older `SCHEMA` is upgraded step by step as `loadGame` loads it, as a mod file is when a player adds it, and as the editor opens it. `upgradePack` does the same for a pack you read yourself. Only a pack from a newer engine is refused.
- `ifloom new --theme none` starts a React UI game with no theme preset, in the UI's plain default look. It works for a copied example too.

### Changed

- The React UI package is now `@ifloom/react` (it was `@ifloom/ui`), and React and Tailwind are only added to games that use them.
- The pages around the game in the React UI (title, settings, load, credits and the gate) are now called pages: `pages`, `usePage`, `TitlePage` and the rest.
- `ifloom check` and `ifloom build` now look for exactly the files the content checker lists.
- `ifloom snapshot` now shows why a choice is refused, as the game does on screen.
- Calendars, places and characters moved out of the core into the calendar, places and cast plugins.
- Notices in the React UI no longer cover the end of the page. The overlay sits after the content and sticks to the bottom of the window, so it floats while you scroll and moves out of the way at the end. On a phone only the newest notice shows, with a `+N more` button (`ui.noticesMore`, `ui.noticesFewer`) for the rest.
- Small Town turns on the keyboard shortcuts and puts the notice strip over the stage, so you can see both in play. Its layout is in `src/layout.ts`.
- Every code example in the docs that draws a game now has a tab for plain JavaScript, React, Vue and Svelte. Pick one and every other page shows the same one, and it's remembered next time. The tutorials, the HUD, plugin UI, minigame, title screen, mods and audio pages show each framework, and the React UI pages say they are about that optional package.
- The docs and the editor on the website now look like the homepage: dark only, with ember colours, the same fonts, glass panels and one dark code theme. The light and dark switch is gone, and the editor previews the React UI's own look in dark.
- Code in the docs now looks like it does in a code editor: VS Code's Dark+ colours on a plain grey panel, with the Plain JS, React, Vue and Svelte tabs drawn as editor tabs and inline code in neutral grey. The sidebar, search button, menus and page buttons have small square corners instead of pills, and the current page is marked with a thin ember bar.
- The homepage's "Start a game" card lets you pick the UI (the React UI, Vue, Svelte or plain JS), an example to start from and a theme preset, and shows the matching command. The plugin list has an icon for each plugin.
- **Breaking:** flags are now the kv store, keys with a true/false, number or text value. `state.flags` is `state.kv` and `FlagValue` is `KvValue`. The condition `{ flag: 'metAva' }` is `{ kv: 'metAva' }`, with the same `is` and ranges. The effect `{ kind: 'flag', key, value }` is `{ kind: 'kv', key, to }`, and `add` is `by`. `storylineFlag` is `storylineKv` (its effect's `value` is `to`), a storyline's and an entity's `flags` are `kv`, `carryOver.flags`, `StartOptions.flags` and the start command's `flags` are `kv`, `SceneCtx.flag` is `SceneCtx.kv`, an input's `variable` is `key`, a stage's `flag` is `key`, `MigrationTools.fill('variables')` is `fill('kv')`, the cast's and generators' `flags` are `kv`, achievements' `{ flag }` progress is `{ kv }`, and the debug plugin's `debug/variable` and `setVariable` are `debug/kv` and `setKv`, taking `to`. Old saves load with their flags in `kv` (save version 12), and old packs and mods are rewritten as they load (schema 5).
- **Breaking:** saves no longer read a `playerName` flag for their `player`. Declare `game.saveName: (state) => string | null` to name saves, as The Letter does.
- **Breaking:** one naming rule for content. An effect or a condition names its target by its noun, `by` changes a number and `to` sets a value. The `stat` effect's `key` is `stat`, and `setStat` is `stat` with `to`: `{ kind: 'stat', stat: 'energy', to: 100 }`. `item` takes `item` (and can now set a count with `to`), `scene` takes `scene`, `minigame` takes `minigame`, `storylineKv` and `closeStoryline` take `storyline` (and `storylineKv` can now change a number `by`), `collect` and `discard` take `collection` (with `discard`'s id as `entry`), and `remove` takes `entity`. `phase`, `audio` and `minigameVar` set with `to` (`audio`'s `to: null` silences a channel), and `minigameVar` adds with `by`.
- **Breaking:** conditions follow the same rule. `{ has: 'scarf' }` is `{ item: 'scarf' }`, `{ turn: true, gte: 3 }` is `{ turn: { gte: 3 } }`, `{ counter: { storyline, key }, gte }` is `{ storylineKv: { storyline, key }, gte }`, `storylineKv`'s `is` sits beside it rather than inside, and a `{ collection }` condition's `id` is `entry`.
- **Breaking:** a command that names one thing names it `id`, and one that sets a value takes `to`. `{ type: 'use', itemId }` is `{ type: 'use', id }`, and `setting` and `input` take `to` instead of `value`. The plugins' commands follow: `places/go`, `cast/talk`, `cast/call`, `quests/track`, `quests/seen`, `jobs/apply`, `jobs/work`, `jobs/quit`, `wardrobe/wear`, `wardrobe/takeOff`, `devices/open`, `devices/close`, `feed/seen`, `messages/read` (for its thread), `debug/scene` and `debug/go` take `id`, and `debug/item` and `debug/turn` take `to`. The helpers (`go`, `talk`, `trackQuest` and the rest) write the new shapes.
- **Breaking:** the plugins' conditions and effects name their target by its noun: `achievements/earned` and `achievements/grant` take `achievement`, `effects/active`, `effects/apply` and `effects/remove` take `effect`, `cast/at` takes `character`, `feed/post` names the new post `post`, and `calendar/pass` takes `by`.
- **Breaking:** one shape for anything that can be refused: `blocked: { when, reason, hide? }[]`, refused while `when` holds, with `hide` to leave the button off the screen. An activity's and a job's `requires` (met while true) are `blocked` the other way round, a place's and an app's `locked` are `blocked`, and choices and actions gain `hide`. `Requirement` is `Blocked`, `need(reason, when)` returns a `blocked` entry that holds while `when` doesn't, and the places plugin's `onlyAt` returns one too.
- Old packs and mods are rewritten to all of the above as they load (schema 6), the editor upgrades projects it saved earlier, and `replay` reads an old command log's commands. Saves are unchanged.
- The editor's Variables section is now "Key–values (kv)", and projects saved by an older editor are upgraded when they open.
- **Breaking:** a game's own custom effects run in order, like plugins' effects. Each is called in its turn with the state the effects before it left, so two in a row compose. Before, they were all worked out up front from the state before the list. Prices, guards and locks still see them worked out against the state on screen (`previewEffects`), and `buildEffects` now passes them on as `{ kind: 'custom', name, with }`, which the long form can also name.
- **Breaking:** custom conditions, effects, values and panels get a context, as plugins' functions do: `(state, args, { game, query, value, roll })`. A custom function can ask a plugin's query or read a plugin's value instead of reaching into `state.plugins`. Custom values take arguments, `(state, args, context)`, and panels take `(state, context)`. `customCondition`, `customEffect`, `customValue` and `customPanel` return the function ready to call.
- **Breaking:** a plugin call's short form always takes its arguments as an object, or `true` for none. `{ 'places/at': ['cafe'] }` is no longer read as `{ value: ['cafe'] }`; `ifloom check` reports it. The first-party calls name what they took: `places/at`, `places/within` and `places/move` take `location`, `cast/here` and `cast/met` take `character`, `calendar/period` takes `period`, `calendar/weekday` takes `day` and `calendar/pass` takes `by`. The helpers (`at`, `here`, `period`, `pass` and the rest) write the new shapes, and the `places/known`, `cast/person` and `cast/where` queries take `location` and `character`.
- **Breaking:** plugins' values take arguments as conditions do, `(state, args, context)`. The messages and feed plugins no longer make a value per channel or feed: `messages/unread-texts` is `messages/unread` with `{ channel: 'texts' }`, and `feed/unseen-social` is `feed/unseen` with `{ feed: 'social' }`. In prose, arguments follow the name: `{{v:messages/unread channel=texts}}`. A devices app's badge, a stat's `max` and an achievement's progress name a value as `{ value, with }`: the badge's `{ count }` is `{ value }`.
- Commands take the short form too: `{ 'places/go': { id: 'cafe' } }` works as an action's or a choice's `command`, and in `apply`, `whyNot`, `effectsOf` and the runner. The view still shows the long form. `CommandRef` is its type and `commandOf` reads one into the long form.
- `loadGame` reports a key a pack can't hold, such as `actions`, as `a pack has no "actions", so it was left out`, instead of dropping it without a word.
- Old packs and mods are rewritten to the above as they load (schema 7): the first-party calls' bare values are named, badges' `count` becomes `value` with its arguments, and value tokens in prose and strings take their arguments.

### Removed

- `validatePack` and `referenced`. Use `checkGame` to check a game's content.
- `migrate`, `migratePlugins`, `migrateGame`, `deserialize`, `serialize` and `hydrate`. Use `loadState` to load a save or any other state from outside.
- The built-in "Wait a while" action. A game declares the actions it offers everywhere in `Game.actions`.

### Fixed

- On a phone, the React UI no longer leaves a gap between the side column and the scene. The page's rows now start at the top instead of stretching to fill the window.
- The terminal preset's status line of notices no longer covers the top of the page. It's now the first row of the page and pushes everything else down, so the scene title, the notice strip and a minigame stay in view. On wide screens it sticks to the top as you scroll.
